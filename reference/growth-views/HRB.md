## The three rates, in the owner's words as recorded on 2026-09-18

Base (2%): Nearly all growth comes from price (+4-5% per return per year). Real volume is roughly flat: the 1-2% company-owned volume growth is partly bought franchise offices, which costs cash rather than creating it; total offices are shrinking; paid DIY customers fell ~4% in FY2026. Assisted is ~65% of revenue growing ~6%, DIY flat, royalties falling, so total revenue grows ~3.5-4%. The main cost is tax-pro wages, which rise about as fast as prices, so cash flow grows slower than revenue and fades as pricing meets resistance. 2% means holding its own in real terms, no better. The CEO's market-share statement gets modest weight: one season, new CEO, no published figures.
Bear (-4%): The AI/free-software fear shows up in the numbers. Simple returns leave, and DIY doesn't catch them because that's where price competition is fiercest. The office network is mostly fixed cost, so a small loss of clients is a larger loss of cash, and raising prices on those who remain speeds up the leaving. -4% compounded leaves about two-thirds of today's cash flow in 10 years: a melting ice cube, not a collapse, because complex returns and people who want a human don't vanish. Management's own 10-K now names AI as a competition risk, and free government filing could return.
Bull (+5%): Share stabilization is real, tax complexity keeps pushing people toward help, pricing holds at 4-5% because remaining clients value the service, AI makes preparers more productive so margins widen, and small-business and financial products add a point. Possible, not probable: it needs both pricing and technology to break the right way for a decade.
The spread is deliberately lopsided (bear 6 points below base, bull 3 above): a mature franchise with flat volume has modest upside and a fatter downside tail.
What would change the view: 2-3 more tax seasons. Same-store assisted volumes holding while prices rise -> base up a point. Client counts falling faster than prices rise -> bear becomes base.
