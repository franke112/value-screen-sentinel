## What Copart does

The world's biggest online auction house for damaged and used cars. When a
car is badly crashed, flooded, or stolen and recovered, the insurer pays
the owner and is left with a wreck it does not want. Copart picks it up,
stores it on its own large yards (it owns the land), and sells it online to
buyers worldwide: repair shops, dismantlers, exporters, rebuilders. It takes
fees from both the seller and the buyer.

Why it is considered a great business:
- **Network effect:** the most buyers means the best prices for insurers,
  which means the most cars, which attracts more buyers.
- **The yards are a moat:** permits for huge wreck lots near cities are
  close to impossible to get today.
- **No customer dependence:** 10-K: *"No single customer accounted for more
  than 10% of our consolidated revenues… and our business does not depend
  on any particular customer."* (Contrast: Cirrus 91% Apple, Maximus ~80%
  Washington.)

## Why it fell (−57% from the May 2025 peak)

1. **Fewer cars written off:** US insurance volumes dropped.
2. **Surprise CEO change:** Jeff Liaw left 31 July, outside the earnings
   cycle; the previous CEO, Jay Adair, came back.
3. **A large purchase:** ACV Auctions (online dealer auctions for used cars)
   at 10.50 USD per share in cash, closing by year-end; a new market.
4. **Analyst downgrades:** e.g. HSBC to Hold, 36 USD target, citing falling
   insurance volumes and softer selling prices.

## Why fewer insurance cars — the owner's questions

**Not fewer crashes; fewer crashes become insurance claims.** US premiums
rose sharply, so people dropped full coverage, raised their deductibles,
and stopped claiming small damage. In Swedish terms: from helförsäkring
with vagnskada down to halvförsäkring or trafikförsäkring. Without
vagnskada the insurer never owns the wreck, so it never reaches Copart; it
gets repaired cheaply or sold locally. Copart loses the car without anyone
choosing a competitor.

**The counter-trend:** new cars are full of sensors and electronics, so even
small crashes make them "not worth repairing". That means more write-offs,
and it is the long-term trend that carried Copart for years.

**The owner's point (friends' payouts were too small to replace the car):**
insurers pay market value (dagsvärde) on the day of the crash, often at the
low end of the market, with the deductible on top. Full cover got worse at
both ends: dearer to hold and less satisfying when used. The session called
this plausible but could not say how much of the effect it explains.
**The twist:** the same high used-car prices raise what each wreck fetches
at auction, so Copart gets fewer cars but more per car. That is part of why
revenue held flat while volumes fell.

## History of large falls

Copart has fallen 25% or more ten times since 1996 and recovered from each
one. (Weekly dividend-adjusted closes; the causes are the session's own
summary, not checked in the data.)

| Fall | When | Size | Cause | Back at the old high |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Dot-com | 2001 → 2003 | −70% | market crash | mid-2004 |
| Financial crisis | 2008 → 2009 | −50% | recession, fuel, scrap prices | end of 2011 |
| Covid | Feb → Mar 2020 | −42% | nobody driving | Aug 2020 |
| Rate shock | 2021 → 2022 | −33% | quality stocks sold off | Apr 2023 |
| **Now** | **May 2025 →** | **−57%** | fewer insurance cars, CEO change, ACV | not yet |

The earlier causes all came **from outside** and passed. This one is **inside
the industry** while the market is fine. Most of the fall is the multiple
(~40× in 2025 by the session's recollection, ~17.6× now), not the profit:
"the business shrank a little; the belief shrank a lot."

What would lift the stock: (1) insurance cars come back, visible every
quarter; (2) the returning CEO and the ACV deal prove themselves; (3) growth
returns and the multiple expands. Meanwhile ~5% FCF yield, no debt and
buybacks. The risk is not collapse but becoming a "normal" company that
never regains its old price.

## The insurance cycle

Why premiums rose in 2022–24: dearer cars, dearer repairs (sensors, labour
shortage), worse crashes after covid, bigger lawsuits ("social
inflation"), and insurers losing money in 2022–23 and repricing hard.
Insurance runs in a cycle: losses → price rises → profits → competition →
price cuts.

Where it stood (2026 sources below): the average full-coverage premium
**fell 6% in 2025**, a record 57% of customers shopped around, and 2026 is
forecast roughly flat (~+1%).

So the thing that should bring cars back started a year ago, yet Copart's
insurance volumes were still falling. Two readings:
- **A — it lags:** people re-cover at renewal, a year or two later. The
  market would then be too pessimistic.
- **B — premiums weren't the whole story:** lasting underinsurance, or
  insurers repairing more. Falling premiums would not save Copart then.

## What the FY2026 10-K itself says

| | FY2026 | FY2025 | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| US service revenue | 3,388 m | 3,452 m | −1.8% |
| International | 581 m | 517 m | +12.4% |
| Total service revenue | 3,970 m | 3,969 m | flat |

Copart's own words: *"The decrease in service revenue in the U.S. was
primarily related to the one-time revenue associated with hurricanes
Helene and Milton recognized in fiscal year 2025 and a decrease in volume
offset by an increase in revenue per car."*

- It says **nothing** about premiums or people dropping cover, so the premium
  theory is neither confirmed nor refuted by Copart.
- It does name a second mechanism: *"…sharp increases in used car prices
  that make it less economical for insurance company sellers to declare a
  vehicle involved in an accident a total loss…"* Expensive used cars make
  insurers repair rather than write off.
- **Buyback:** 1.6 bn USD in FY2026, 43.4 m shares at an average 37.63 USD,
  the first in years. Management put real money behind "cheap", and is ~28%
  under water at 27.22.
- Costs rose (yards, administration) and profit dipped slightly.

## What to watch

**Q1 FY2027 (Aug–Oct 2026, reported around late November)** is the first
quarter with no Helene/Milton comparison. The one number: **does U.S. service
revenue / insurance volume still fall without the hurricane comparison?**
Wildcard: a big hurricane this autumn would lift volumes temporarily.

## Where it ended

The owner liked the business ("this is looking better and better… im
interested in this company"). The store was written (b017235), the growth
view registered (6% / 1% / 10%), and the strike gave fv_base 25.45 against
27.22. **The owner PASSED: a good business at a price above the owner's own base
case.** It stays INTAKE, with an Avanza alert at 22 USD and a revisit at the
November report. See the watchlist entry and
