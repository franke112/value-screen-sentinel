## Value across the cycle (r 9.5 %, shares 441m, net debt ex. leases about 265 after the issue, USDNOK 9.23)

FCF0 = EBITDA - tax 50 - leases 360 - minorities 85 - fleet renewal. Fleet
renewal is an *est*: 81 owned vessels at about 100m each over about 27 years
is about 300m a year, plus a margin for the fleet growth the company is
ordering, so 300-500m. Trough-year capex of about 140m is not a guide: the
fleet aged and the company is now paying for it (1.4bn on order).

**Verdict: no.** It fails Q1 and Q2. On the cycle mean its value is about
90-140 NOK against 171. The price is fair only if the 2026 guide holds for
good, and cheap only if 2024's peak returns. 2026 EBITDA is already running
about 15 % under 2025, 60 new car carriers enter the world fleet in 2026,
and the company is about to spend 1.4bn on its own newbuilds.

**What the owner's 2020 buy got right is not this method.** At 22 NOK in
November 2020 the company was valued at about 0.43x book (equity to owners
2 391m USD at end-2020 x USDNOK 9.05 / 423m shares = about 51 NOK a share) at
a loss-making trough. Today it is about 2.1x book (3 660m at Q2 2026 + the
300m issue = about 83 NOK a share). A shipping company is bought at the bottom of the cycle against its
book and fleet value, and sold at the top. A DCF on normal years cannot see
the bottom, and at 171 NOK it is not the bottom. The book-value check that
would flag a trough is not in the framework, and this note does not add it
(owner's rule: framework changes only when a name is blocked).
