## 2. SAP.DE did NOT resolve by the 13 MEUR bound, and the bound was wrong

The handover asked for a bound of 13. **Confirming the columns is what showed
it could not stand.** The 20-F's cash flow statement (F-8, *Consolidated
Statements of Cash Flows of SAP Group for the Years Ended December 31*) has
columns **2025 / 2024 / 2023**, and the line reads **−2 / −1 / −13** — so 13
is FY2023. The §5 basis is the TTM to 2026-06-30, and **none of those three
years covers it.**

SAP prints the line every quarter, and the window is:

```
2025-Q3   Q1-Q3 2025  -3   less  Q1-Q2 2025   0   =   -3    (E86 operands)
2025-Q4   Q1-Q4 2025  -2   less  Q1-Q3 2025  -3   =   +1
2026-Q1   Q1    2026  -5   stated, a first-quarter column IS the quarter
2026-Q2   Q1-Q2 2026 -28   less  Q1    2026  -5   =  -23
                                                     ----
                                                      -30 MEUR
```

**H1 2026 alone is −28**, past the proposed bound. So the leg is the FIGURE,
not a bound: −30,000,000 EUR, 0.41% of FCF0, and the 20-F's FY2025 tag
(`ifrs-full:DividendsPaidToNoncontrollingInterestsClassifiedAsFinancing‑
Activities`, 2,000,000, accession 0001104659-26-020058) is a check on it
rather than the figure.

## 4. E108, the sensitivity floor, and what it does to KAR.ST

Three limbs. **Zero limb** (no valuation needed): ±10% of zero is zero.
**Bound limb** (no growth view needed): where the bridge is net cash,
`|Δfv/fv| ≤ 10% × |leg| / |FCF0|` for every growth view, because
`fv × shares = D(g)×FCF0 + net_cash` and adding a non-negative constant to
the denominator can only shrink the ratio. **Measured limb**: the record
valued three times per leg.

**KAR.ST: 63 blocking figures before, 58 after — the floor clears 5.**
(It was 59 before E105 added four zeros of its own; those four are among the
five cleared, with `2026-Q2 prepaid_delivery_obligation`.)

It clears no more, and the reason is structural rather than a shortfall:

* **the bridge is NET DEBT** (2,005.7 MSEK), so no growth-free bound exists —
  with `net_cash < 0` the factor exceeds 1 without limit as the DCF shrinks;
* **no growth view is pre-registered for KAR.ST** (E28), so there is no
  `fv_base` to measure 1% of, and the measured limb cannot run either.

What CAN be said without a growth view, because for a net-debt name the leg's
share of FCF0 is a **lower** bound on the move (FCF0 252.2 MSEK):

| leg | lower bound on the ±10% move | verdict |
|---|---:|---|
| `operating_cash_flow` (×4) | ≥ 14.87% | **can never be exempt** |
| `capex_ppe` / `capex_intangibles` (×8) | ≥ 7.36% | **can never be exempt** |
| `net_interest_paid` (×4) | ≥ 2.88% | **can never be exempt** |
| `sbc` (×1) | ≥ 0.39% | undecidable without the view |

**16 of KAR.ST's 58 are legs the floor could never reach at any effort.** The
remaining 42 are ranking-key and per-share fields the floor does not reach at
all. KAR.ST needs a read-back, not a tolerance.

## 8. E106 clause 3 is storable, and it needed a DIRECTION

`bound:` and `bound_direction:` on a figure. A bound is a MAGNITUDE, needs
a page (refused at the load without one), and never sits beside a value.

**The first implementation perturbed every bounded leg downward**, which is
right for E105's deduction and wrong for E70's add-back: a leg struck at
zero that can only be an add-back has all its plausible values ABOVE the
struck one. E106 clause 1's own words — *"on ANY PLAUSIBLE VALUE"* — settle
it, so it was applied rather than raised. The two directions are summed
**separately** and the larger absolute move taken.

Fixed on the way: `legs()` asked `missing()`, `missing()` asks
`tolerance()`, `tolerance()` values the record and that asks `legs()`. The
cycle only closed the first time a record actually carried a bounded leg.

## 9. AOS — bounded, measured, and still refused

A. O. Smith tags no `us-gaap:OperatingLeasePayments` and prints no
supplemental cash-flow lease line. **No search can make E70's leg present.**
What note 4 states does bound it:

| stated in note 4 | |
|---|---:|
| Operating lease expense, cost of products sold | 6.4 |
| Operating lease expense, SG&A | 16.8 |
| **total operating lease expense FY2025** | **23.2** |
| *of which short-term lease expense* | *1.8* |
| *of which variable lease expense* | *5.8* |
| maturities: 2026 / total undiscounted / PV | 14.3 / 56.7 / 47.9 |

23.2 is a strict ceiling on what E70 adds back, for two stated reasons: the
note says *"Cash flows associated with leases are materially consistent with
the expense recorded in the consolidated statement of earnings"*, and 23.2
**includes** the short-term and variable costs that ASC 842 keeps out of the
lease liability — so it exceeds the payments on the capitalised portfolio,
which are the only ones E70 adds back.

**23.2 moves `fv_base` by 4.27%. E106's limit is 3.5%. AOS REFUSES** —
clause 2, exactly as before the ruling. What changed is the quality of the
refusal: it used to read *a leg is absent*; it now reads *the leg is worth
at most 4.27% of the answer, and that is too much.*

**The tighter ceiling, 23.2 − 1.8 − 5.8 = 15.6, would clear it at about
2.9%, and was not taken.** Every operand is printed on the same page, but
the operation TIGHTENS a bound, and a mistake in a tightening step lets a
valuation run that should not — the same asymmetry E35 uses to require a
liability leg and not an asset one. Whether a bound may be tightened by
arithmetic on stated figures is not answered by any ruling, and it is item 1
below.
