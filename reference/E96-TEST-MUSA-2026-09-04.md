# Is Murphy USA inside E96? — tested 2026-09-04, before any view was registered

**The answer is NO, and the filing settles it in one sentence.**

The owner put the question because he described the business as *"olja, gas,
bensinmackar"*, and E96 removes at STEP 0 any company **whose revenue is a
world market price it does not set, multiplied by a volume.**

## What E96's mechanism actually is

> *for such a business **the bear case is not slower growth — it is a halved
> price**, and a halved price is not a lower `g` on the same cash flow but a
> different cash flow altogether. §5 discounts **a durable free cash flow
> that such a company does not have.***

The test is about the **cash flow**, not about the word "oil". A producer
*receives* the world price; its cash flow is that price times a volume.

## What Murphy USA says it does, in its own words

> **"We purchase fuel from oil companies, independent refiners, and other
> marketers at rates that fluctuate with market prices and generally are
> reset daily, and we sell fuel to our customers at prices that WE ESTABLISH
> DAILY."**
> — 10-K 0001573516-26-000090, Item 1

**That is E96's mechanism inverted.** The world price is Murphy USA's
**COST**, not its revenue. What it earns is the spread between a price it
pays and a price it sets.

And the direction of the exposure is the opposite of a producer's:

> **"Dramatic increases in oil prices reduce retail gasoline gross margins,
> because wholesale gasoline costs typically increase faster than retailers
> are able to pass them along to customers."**
> — 10-K 0001573516-26-000090, Item 7A

A producer is hurt by a **falling** price. Murphy USA is hurt by a **rising**
one. A halved crude price halves the revenue LINE and does not halve the
cash flow — which is exactly why the revenue line is not the test.

## Where the money actually comes from, FY2025

| | $m |
|---|---:|
| Petroleum product sales | 14,862.8 |
| *less* petroleum product cost of goods sold | (13,589.8) |
| **fuel contribution (a spread it sets)** | **1,273.0** |
| Merchandise sales | 4,303.8 |
| merchandise margin, 20.2% of sales | **~869** |
| Total revenues | 19,384.0 |

**About 41% of the gross contribution never touches a fuel price at all**,
and the other 59% is a margin rather than a price. The filer runs **one
reporting segment, called Marketing** (10-K, Item 1: *"Our business is
organized into one reporting segment (Marketing)"*).

## E96 already carved this out by name

E96's own list of **WHAT IS EXPRESSLY NOT SWEPT IN** reads:

> *Integrated and midstream oil and gas. An integrated major carries
> **refining and marketing margins that move against the crude price**, and a
> midstream operator is often a toll on volume under contract. **Neither is
> the fault this rule names.***

Murphy USA is the **marketing** half of that sentence, standing alone. Its
margins move against the crude price, and the filing says so in those words.

## Both membership limbs also miss it, which is corroboration and not the test

* **the string limb** — the vendor classifies MUSA `Consumer Cyclical`, and
  `config/screener_commodity_price.yaml` carries no sector at all, on
  purpose;
* **the ticker limb** — the owner's list is empty.

Neither limb reaching a name proves nothing on its own; **the substance
above is the answer**, and the limbs agree with it.

## Conclusion

**Murphy USA is OUTSIDE E96.** A growth view may be registered and §5 may be
run. Recorded before either happened.

**What would move it back inside**, so a later reader can check rather than
re-argue: if the company's cash flow ever became a world price times a
volume — a move into production, or a supply arrangement that fixed its
retail price to a benchmark it does not set. Neither is true of the FY2025
filing.
