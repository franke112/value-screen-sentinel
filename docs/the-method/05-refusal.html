Why a name is refused, and why that is the normal outcome

The claimMost companies that are read are refused, a company that fails on a fact about its business stays refused at any price, and 'I cannot judge this' is a real answer.

About 9 minutes · the investor's route.

By the time a name reaches a person, it has survived a coarse screen. It is cheap-ish, it makes money, it has not obviously broken. The temptation is to think the hard part is done. It is not. The reading is where most names are refused, and refusal is the expected result, not a disappointment.

The gate that does most of the refusing: why did the price fall?

The screen passes companies without asking why the price is where it is. The reader's first job is to name the reason for any fall, and put it in one of four boxes.

  • A: the mood changed. Nothing about the business changed; the story people tell about it did.
  • B: the whole sector was sold. Money left an industry and took this company with it.
  • C: a one-off cost with a number on it. A fine, a write-down, a factory fire. It happened once and its size is known.
  • D: something is actually broken. A product that no longer sells, a customer that left, a regulator that has changed the rules for good.

A, B and C pass. D fails, and the rule is stricter than it looks: if the reader cannot say with confidence which box it is, it is D.11Framework §3 gate 2: four disconnect classes; 'if you cannot classify confidently, it is D.' A real name was dropped on class D on 2026-09-08. Not knowing why a price fell is not neutral. It is the most common way to buy a broken business.

Worked example: NAME A

NAME A passed the screen. Its price is 35 percent below last year's high, which the screen only notes. The reader opens the last four reports. Revenue is flat. The fall began the week the company's largest customer, a fifth of its sales, announced it would make the product itself from next year. That is a lost customer that is not coming back: box D. NAME A fails gate 2 and leaves. Its cheapness was never the question.

The other gates

A name that passes the "why" gate is then tested on its accounts over the last two years: debt within a stated multiple of earnings, interest covered at least five times by operating profit, spare cash generated in at least six of the last eight quarters, no auditor's doubt about the company continuing, no change of auditor, no restated accounts. The debt-multiple limb is information now: it is printed as quality test Q3 and decides nothing (E168). Then it needs a dated event within ninety days that could make the market look again: a results date, a product decision, a ruling. "Eventually the market will realise" is not an event.22Framework §3 gates 3 and 5. Gate 4, a valuation discount against peers, is recorded as DATA MISSING for every name because the peer figures it needs have never been assembled; it is not deleted, and returns when they exist (ruling E99).

Then there are four hard kills: things that end the reading on their own, whatever else looks good. Two or more cuts to the company's own forecast in four quarters. Three or more missed earnings in eight quarters. Being close to breaking a lending agreement. Both the chief executive and the finance chief leaving within twelve months. Until November 2026 there were seven. Falling revenue, squeezed margins and swelling stock or unpaid invoices went out with ruling E163, and the debt-multiple leg with E168. The tests behind that are in chapter 13.33Framework §4.2. 'Any one invalidates.'

Why a failed gate is final

The natural way, which is wrong

NAME A failed on a lost customer at a price of 60. Six months later the price is 40. Surely at 40 it is worth another look? The price has done half the work.

The rule, and why

The price was never the problem. NAME A failed on a fact about its business, and at 40 that fact is still true. A name that failed on something other than price is parked as WATCH-GATED, and the only thing that can bring it back is a named new fact: a new filing, a changed forecast, a warning. A price level cannot reopen it, because a price alert would wake it into the same gate it is already known to fail.

This is written into the method as a ruling with a number, and it is the reason the tool keeps two kinds of waiting list apart. A name that is merely too expensive waits for a price. A name that failed a gate waits for a fact.44Ruling E27 splits the old single 'watch' status into WATCH-PRICED and WATCH-GATED; the retired spelling is refused by the code with a message pointing at the ruling.

The same finality applies to the rules themselves. When two limbs of a gate were found to measure nothing useful, they were removed, and the ruling that removed them says "not widened, not softened, removed". A rule is either in force or it has been retired by a dated decision. There is no third state where it is bent for a name that nearly passed.55Ruling E30, 2026-08-25, deleting two limbs of gate 3 that measured the volatility of reported figures rather than the quality of the business, having changed no verdict across five names.

The circle: refusing what you cannot judge

Some businesses are refused before any gate, not because they are bad but because the method cannot be run on them honestly. The method needs the owner to write down a believable range for how the business will grow (chapter 8). For some businesses that is not a judgement anyone can make.

  • A company whose revenue is a world price it does not set, multiplied by a volume: a miner, an oil producer, a bulk shipper. Its bad case is not slower growth but a halved price, which is a different business, not a lower number in the same arithmetic.66Ruling E96, 2026-08-31: 'This is a limit of the METHOD, and it is not a judgement about the industry.' Sixteen industry patterns; regulated utilities, contracted output and semiconductors expressly excluded from the rule.
  • A drug developer, whose growth is a guess about approvals the owner has no way to assess.77Ruling E51.

Buffett's phrase for this is the circle of competence: "The size of that circle is not very important; knowing its boundaries, however, is vital."88Buffett, 1996 letter to shareholders. What this project adds is that the boundary is written down as a rule and enforced by the screen, so that a spectacularly cheap miner cannot tempt anyone into pretending the method applies. Such a name is never watched at all, not even on its price, because there is no level it could reach that would mean anything.99Ruling E97: a name outside the circle 'is NEVER WATCHED AT ALL, not even on its drawdown'.

Refusing a business you cannot judge is not a cop-out. It is the only answer that does not require pretending.

The exact rules

Framework §3 (gates), §4.2 (hard kills), §4.4 (the quality tests, information only). Rulings E161 to E168 (the price band, the kills, the quality tests as information), E27 (the two waiting lists), E30 (deletion of two limbs), E51 and E96 (the circle), E97 (never watched), E99 (gate 4 as DATA MISSING). Code: the status list in vss/rules.py, the circle lists under config/.

CENTA at this step

CENTA was not refused. It was bought first and tested afterwards, which is the order this chapter exists to prevent. When the quality tests were run on 27 October it failed four of five: a return of 12.6 percent on the capital in the business against a bar of 15; one year, 2022, in which the owners' earnings were negative; net debt of 4.5 times its spare cash against a limit of 3; and a return of 4.9 percent on the companies it had bought, against the 9.5 percent the method demands. Under the rule of that day, a name that fails is sold. Chapter 13 says what later became of that rule.

What the test said

Seven warning signs once ended a reading on their own. Three were tested and removed (attempts 17 and 24): falling sales, a squeezed margin and swelling unpaid invoices did not separate the companies that did well from the others. A fourth lost its debt leg (attempt 25). Of the signs that could be tested, only repeated earnings misses separated returns. Cuts to a company's own forecast and the loss of both top executives could not be tested, for lack of data, and stay.

In three lines
  1. The first question is why the price fell. If you cannot say, treat the business as broken.
  2. Four warning signs end a reading on their own.
  3. A name that failed on something other than price comes back only on a named new fact, never on a lower price.
